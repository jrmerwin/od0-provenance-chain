# 5. Part 2 — Renewal structure and late-regime growth law

## 5.1 Renewal at F = 0

Prove that the process regenerates at drained states (F = 0) modulo the DAG: the next burst is determined by the served subset alone, with S^V = min(Γ, D). Define cycle = excursion of F from 0; burst size, burst cost, drain length as exact functions of the cycle. Give the exact distribution of burst size at a drained state as a function of (Γ, X) (from R52 §4.3 and the composite graph).

## 5.2 Growth law (theorem or bounds)

Under constant total capacity (per §4.1 carry-forward), prove one of:

- **(U) unbounded growth**: |X_k| → ∞ almost surely, with explicit rate bounds. Registered target: Θ(log k), from geometric growth of burst cost with depth against linear drain; give the constants in terms of (Γ, c_first, c_repeat) if obtainable, else two-sided bounds.
- **(T) almost-sure termination**: growth stops at a finite random size; characterize the terminal law or bound it.
- **(P) partial**: exact bounds not closing (U) vs (T); state the gap precisely.

Use the R52 global bound P(S^V ≥ 2 | state) > 0 and the drain dynamics (forced served ≤ Γ + relief per step). Exact statements only; label any heuristic as `CONJECTURE`.

## 5.3 Drain-length scaling

Prove or bound: the expected drain length after a burst of cost C is C/(Γ + relief-rate) up to exact corrections; the ratio of successive cycle lengths along a trajectory grows (geometric) — exact statement or bounds.

## 5.4 Sensitivity to the frozen constitutive rules
